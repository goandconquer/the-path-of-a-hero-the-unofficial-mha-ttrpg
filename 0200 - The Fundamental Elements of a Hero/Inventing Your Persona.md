# Inventing Your Persona

> "If you feel yourself hitting up against your limit, remember for what cause you clench your fists. Remember why you started down this path, and let that memory carry you beyond your limit."
> 
> — All Might, *My Hero Academia*

The **Inventing Your Persona** section covers the contextual in-story elements as they relate to your understanding of the character and their relationship to the world around them and the story they will take part in.

Before you dive into the mechanical concepts that define *what* your character is according to *The Path of a Hero* system, you should consider *who* your character is.

At this point, you won't see very much in the way of the concepts and mechanics of character creation, but you will likely find the mechanical processes far more enjoyable once you've read and considered these brainstorming topics that are very relevant to the unfolding of your character since they will shape what mechanical areas you consider fundamental to the unfolding of your character through play.

## Backgrounds

While anyone can find themselves with the powers and responsibilities granted by the manifestation of a Quirk, there is no greater indicator of the kind of story they will have than with where they started.

**Backgrounds** lay the foundation for how your character's personality, motivations, morality, and goals align with the momentum of the ever-changing world they reside in. Some characters may find themselves soaring confidently into the fray, eager to face the dangers the day presents them. Other characters may struggle to reconcile their current lives with the emergence of their newfound powers. 

As a player, your first major decision is to decide what circumstances your character found themselves in at the time of their Quirk awakening. Does the harshness of their childhood lead them to see their powers as a means to exact revenge on a society that formerly saw them as expendable? Does a thoughtful upbringing push them to uphold the virtues of the heroic culture of the today, even when it is inconvenient?

When considering the background of your character, consider how their lineage, environments, and the philosophies of those around them could contribute to their future ambitions and actions. Backgrounds that provide enough malleable creative material that both challenges your character and yet still inspires them are often the strongest as their relationship with this context will often expand and evolve throughout the lifespan of the narrative.

Backgrounds also largely influence a character by revealing how much monetary wealth is available to them to support their transition from the life of an ordinary citizen to a role much greater.  

The following backgrounds serve as starting points for your brainstorming. They are not intended to be followed strictly as they are written, rather you should be adding and removing as much as you see fit as you design the character you want to play. In cases where your proposed background deviates widely from those found here, you should consult with your gamemaster to determine how much wealth is available to your character as a result.

### Ex-Delinquent

You have been in and out of trouble with the law throughout your life. You have a certain amount of "street cred" and may or may not have a few contacts in the underworld. You are more of an anti-hero and looked down upon by the hero community, but you get some dirtier jobs done that others cannot.

A character with the Ex-Delinquent background receives 3d4 * 30 starting Quin.

### Projects Kid

You come from a lesser income family and have lived with less all your life. You understand the worth of money and know where some villains are coming from, having a similar background to many low-level thugs. Despite your lack of affluence, hard work, and hard work, and perseverance has gotten you closer to your dreams and the money you always lacked as a child.

A character with the Project Kid background receives 3d4 * 40 starting Quin.

### Heroic Parents

You come from parents who either make more money than average or have parents who are active heroes in the community and have an above average income due to that. With this type of lifestyle, you will have more responsibility put onto your shoulders. You see becoming a hero and beginning a legacy as the best way to honor your family.

A character with the Heroic Parents background receives 5d4 * 60 starting Quin.

### Legendary Heroic Lineage

You are simply the newest member in a long line of heroes, members of your family are extremely high-ranking heroes or very wealthy. This puts an extremely heavy burden on you to become every bit as impressive as those before you. The way that you have decided to do this is through heroism.

A character with the Legendary Hero Lineage background receives 5d4 * 80 starting Quin.

### Villainous Heritage

You have become a ward of the state due to the actions of one or more of your parents or ancestors. The government has done their best to hide this background to allow you a normal life. Despite their best efforts, a combination of Quirk inheritance, as well as information leaks, makes it difficult for you to remain out of the public eye. You plan to overcome this by proving to everyone that you can be a better hero than anyone.

A character with the Villainous Heritage background receives 50 starting Quin.

## Psyches

A **Psyche** is the profile of enduring characteristics and behavior that guide your character as they are influenced by and respond to the conceptual and narrative elements of the world they live in, especially through their thoughts, words, and actions.

Your character's psyche can be defined as simply as the primary emotions that your character defaults to when faced with opposition, or as thoroughly as the tenants of morality that your character abides by, the extent to which they expect the world to honor those tenants, and what it would take for them to break those tenants.

Psyches are the most abstract element of the character creation process, as the decisions may seemingly come across as impulsive or without a clear reason why a particular notion of the character's personality is right or wrong. However, their conception can provide insight into the other major choices associated with the character creation process, especially in regards to the kind of Quirk that manifests within them (if they have one).

When determining the Psyche of your character, you should consider what virtues are represented most strongly by your character.

Is your character more imaginative or insightful? Is your character multi-faceted, naturally gifted, or a slow learner? How curious are they? How fearful are they? Do they value knowledge over experiences? Do they value control? Are they patient and thoughtful, able to sense out the hidden dangers around them, or are they ambitious to a fault? Are they fiercely independent, or are they easily led astray? Are they socialites or loners? Are they enduring in trust and kindness, or are they paranoid of being betrayed? Are they blossoming allies or bitter rivals? Are they self-sacrificing or manipulative? Are they resilient or easily overwhelmed?

All these questions impact how your character will face the world and how the world will face them.

### Aspirations

A character with no goals has no reason to be a character. In a setting writhe with competition and uncertainty, you will want to define certain targets that generally motivate your characters to faces the dangers of the day.

An **Aspiration** is any articulated desire that can only be obtained through the diligent action of the character who desires it. The aspirations of your character may be quite personal to them, or they may represent a larger, more public ambition that requires the collective efforts of other people or even other organizations to realize. They may be secretive and jeopardize either the safety or the acceptance of your character, or they may be issues that should see the light of public scrutiny.

In any case, your character's chosen aspirations should also intrinsically suggest obstacles they are not yet able to be overcome, whether due to a lack of experience, authority, or resources. It is not necessary that these all be long term goals, although a good aspiration is one that has the potential to evolve and to color moments throughout the lifespan of the narrative.

As you determine who your character is as a person, you should consider how their aspirations might unite or divide others around them. You should consider what sacrifices might be required of your character, and whether they are in a place to fully realize them. 

Aspirations that drive a character forward throughout their journey while remaining an open question about commitment can create rewarding drama to explore throughout the process of roleplay.

### Flaws and Fears

**Flaws and fears** are the internal elements of your character that impede the realization of their aspirations. This differs from the likely external obstacles that their ambitions invite as they cannot be identified and solved by anyone other than the character themselves.

Good flaws and fears are those that invite the gamemaster to interweave them into the expanding narrative through scenes, problems, or moments of roleplay. Additionally, they should be potentially conquerable.

The exact nature of their conquering does not necessarily have to be determined at this stage of the process but having a general sense of how your might your character conquer their fears and flaws is a powerful influence on the achievement of their aspirations and the strengthening of their psyche.

### Likes and Dislikes

The **likes and dislikes** of a character are the internal elements of your character which allow them to come across as more believable and relatable.

These elements can be as simple as their favorite color and be as elaborate and detailed as the exact specifications of their ideal life. The point, in any case, is so that you have a collection of character-centric details that you can reveal to your fellow players as hooks for their engagement with you. The day-to-day life of your character is just as integral to the narrative as are the dramatic events of the world around them. 

## Personas

A **Persona** is the outward identity that your character assumes as a means of maintaining the privacy of their day-to-day life as well as visibly communicating the sort of ideals and strengths they represent as a Quirk wielder.

This concept should also help you to discern how truly public or private your character is in the eyes of the society they are a participant of. Do they try everything in their power to preserve the separation between hero and citizen? Do they bank all of their time, effort, and attention to the service of the ideals represented by their persona? Do they use their persona as a means of accruing praise and recognition in ways that they could never do without it?

While it may seem trivial, the definition of your character's persona has as much to do with your character's psyche as do their internal elements. A good persona is one which claims something that might be controversial or defiant in the broader social context.

### Causes

**Causes** are, broadly speaking, the reasons for why your character does what they do through the use of their persona. These are public-facing and they are actionable and they are capable of being scrutinized.

Causes can be as simple as vowing to uphold the natural virtues of heroism, or advocating on behalf of a repressed social ground, perhaps even the Quirk-wielding community itself, or as complex as pronouncing dedication to the upheaval or eradication of a part of the way of life for society.

These causes will largely influence how the world sees your character.

### Branding

**Branding** is the representational aspects of your character's appearance, behavior, and action which ultimately communicate that your character is devoted to their causes. It can be an artistic process or a philosophical one but the general premise behind branding as a consideration again reflects exactly how into or out of the public sphere your character desires to be.

Largely, branding deals also with how your characters wants to be talked about by the general public. It can include the alias they assume to hide their true identity, the symbols, colors, or designs they choose to represent themselves or their causes, or simply those habits which allow outside observers to distinguish your character from others.

### Outfitting

**Outfitting** is an aspect of branding but it can also be a mechanical consideration based upon the nature of the Quirk you envision your character to have.

In some cases, specialized outfits or accessories are needed for a character to safely co-exist with their Quirk. These things could also help a character to bridge the gap between a haphazard utility and a focused ability, thereby improving the odds that they could help others with it. Likewise, gadgets could be used to amplify an otherwise weak Quirk to relatively potent new heights for your character.

Regardless of their needs, it is often a defining moment for a character to assume their costume. Therefore, you might prepare for this moment by thinking about how these elements communicate the inner world of your character.
